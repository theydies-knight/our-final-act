# The Realm of Thay:<br> Land of Shadow, Magic, & Intrigue

## 1. Introduction: The Realm of Red Wizards

### 1.1 Visual and Environmental Aesthetic

Rising dramatically above the surrounding eastern realms upon massive, elevated stone plateaus, the land of Thay presents a breathtaking yet terrifying fantasy landscape. Its grand architecture synthesizes opulent Moroccan aesthetics with the canal-laced, elegant urban design of early Renaissance Venice—a high-fantasy Mediterranean landscape situated high above the surrounding world. Soaring, obsidian spires capped with glowing illusory waterfalls and suspended magical gardens pierce the artificial sky, overlooking vibrant open-air bazaars, marble plazas, and arched aqueducts spanning deep waterways.

> _"Beneath the gleaming spires and sunlit canals of Thay lies a realm built on dark arcana and absolute subjugation. Here, magic is not merely a tool—it is the supreme law, counter-intelligence is a lifestyle, and mercy is a lethal vulnerability."_

Yet, beneath this breathtaking exterior lies an omnipresent, suffocating tension. Crimson clouds drift across artificially controlled skies, armored enforcers patrol cobblestone streets alongside bound fiends, and masked mages whisper conspiratorial plots in suspended aerial meeting chambers. It is a world of vibrant high fashion, staggering artistic achievement, and relentless, sub-rosa espionage.

### 1.2 Wealth, Power, & Oppression

Understanding Thay requires grasping its fundamental paradox: it is simultaneously one of the wealthiest, most magically advanced nations in Faerûn and one of the most ruthless magocracies in existence. Through the corrupt Guild of Foreign Trade and vast international networks of trade enclaves, Thay dominates the global magic item market, churning out mass-produced arcane wares.

However, this economic dominance and architectural magnificence sit atop a brutal fascist caste system. Thay is ruled by a tyrant class of powerful spellcasters known as the Red Wizards. In this society, common folk and those lacking arcane talent possess zero recognized legal rights. They exist at the absolute mercy of ruthless magocrats who punish any perceived defiance with immediate execution, soul binding, or forced labor in magic item fabrication facilities.

For starting player characters entering the campaign at level 11, this oppressive atmosphere is sharpened by immediate urgency. The 400-year-old lich Szass Tam, Zulkir of Necromancy, openly declared his intention to reclaim his title as Regent. As such, he would claim ultimate authority over Thay. Prudence borne by those amongst the Open Eyes would seek ulterior motives. Alas, the lich's surveillance networks actively hunt political dissidents. Survival in this setting demands mastery of spycraft, cover identities, surveillance tradecraft, and lethal arcane combat.

To navigate this gilded cage of magocratic tyranny without drawing the gaze of state informants, operatives must first master the strict hierarchies that govern Thayan life.

## 2. The Magocratic Hierarchy: Who Rules Thay?

### 2.1 The Red Wizards

At the absolute apex of Thayan society stand the Red Wizards—scheming mages who firmly believe it is their divine destiny to conquer and rule the known world. Their word is supreme law, backed by state power. The only entity legally capable of overruling a Red Wizard is a more powerful Red Wizard.

To navigate Thayan society effectively, operatives must distinguish between public legal markers (_What Thayans Know_) and the internal worldviews that drive Red Wizard behavior (_Red Wizard Hallmarks_):

#### Red Wizard Hallmarks (Internal Mindsets)

- **Axiom of Superiority:** Red Wizards view all non-magical individuals as inherent inferiors, fit only for servitude or resource extraction.
- **Common Folk Are Servants:** Mages expect absolute, immediate compliance from anyone without economic or arcane power.
- **Paramount Ambition:** Personal empowerment and political enrichment are viewed as sacred rights, provided the mage cloaks their actions in service to Thay.
- **Malleable Loyalty:** Oaths sworn to Zulkirs or masters are kept only as long as politically advantageous. Betrayal and covert assassination are considered standard methods of career advancement.
- **Inevitability of Supremacy:** Every Red Wizard operates under the core belief that they possess the potential to rise to the Council of Zulkirs through sufficient ruthlessness and guile.

#### What Thayans Know (Public Markers & Rules)

Less frequently enforced laws prohibit violent crimes and lesser offenses such as property damage. Punishments range from fines or imprisonment to death, at Red Wizards' whims.

- **Mandatory Red Attire:** Red Wizards wear exclusive crimson robes. Wearing red clothing is strictly outlawed for anyone else in Thay, serving as an absolute visual marker of state authority.
- **Shaved and Tattooed Heads:** Red Wizards shave their heads and cover them in intricate tattoos. Some tattoos denote noble lineage or loyalty to a Zulkir, while magical spell tattoos actively augment their arcane power.
- **Powerful Mages:** Red Wizards are master spellcasters who swear loyalty to higher-ranking members of their order.
- **Unquestioned Authority:** Disrespecting a Red Wizard is legally classified as high treason. Mages mete out harsh summary judgments at their personal discretion.
- **Cruel and Self-Centered:** Common folk avoid direct contact with Red Wizards, knowing an imperial mage's personal whims can destroy an entire family's livelihood in an instant.
- **Absolute Binding Rulings:** A Red Wizard's legal decision is final. Commoners have no court of appeal. If Red Wizards give contradictory commands, the highest-ranking Red Wizard must be obeyed.

#### Zulkirs' Resources

Thay's zulkirs accumulate enormous resources prior to ascending to the council. All have numerous streams of income, often from finding or creating and later selling magical treasures. The zulkirs each own at least one home in the Zulkirs' Ward in Eltabbar, Thay's capital city, and in Thaymount, where protective magic helps them keep their biggest secrets away from prying eyes. Zulkirs also maintain legions of staff, soldiers, and other servants.

### 2.3 Civil Administration: Tharchions & Autharchs

While the Council of Zulkirs dictates grand national policy, warfare, and foreign trade, daily civil governance and counter-intelligence operations are delegated through a dense bureaucracy:

- **Tharchs & Tharchions:** Thay is divided geographically into eleven administrative provinces called **tharchs**. Each tharch is governed autonomously by a **tharchion** appointed by the Council. Most tharchions are former military commanders who manage local taxation, infrastructure, garrison forces, and provincial security.
- **Autharchs:** Tharchions delegate local municipal duties to **autharchs**—lesser civil functionaries serving as mayors, magistrates, tax collectors, and station chiefs. Autharchs exist under extreme stress: incompetent autharchs are executed, while overly capable autharchs are routinely purged before becoming political threats to their governors.

### 2.4 Thayan Knights & Private Armies

To enforce their will and maintain personal security, high-ranking Red Wizards employ **Thayan Knights**. These martial champions shave their heads, wear decorative facial and head tattoos, and swear binding lifetime oaths of personal service to their wizard patrons. Wearing heavy armor and wielding spell-augmented weaponry, a Thayan Knight acts as an elite personal bodyguard and military captain.

Thay lacks a unified, centralized army. Instead, the nation relies on private military forces raised, equipped, and garrisoned by individual Red Wizards or Tharchions. These armies directly reflect the arcane specializations of their masters: Evokers deploy elemental shock troops, Illusionists rely on tactical trickery and lethal ambushes, and Szass Tam commands the infamous **Dread Legion**—a terrifying war machine composed of skeletal legionnaires, fiends, and undead warriors led by Death Knights Slethisz Vendrein and Hazrash Kulmantle.

While this top-heavy political structure maintains absolute control through arcane and martial power, the common populace has developed distinct survival strategies to endure beneath it.

### 2.5 Thayan Wealth

Thay's economy runs off heavy taxes and the export of magic items. Though many of its residents are poor, Thay is one of the richest nations in Faerûn.

#### Tax Stations

Small fortresses called tax stations pepper Thay's border, roads, and internal boundaries. Soldiers at each tax station collect tolls imposed by the controlling tharchion. Tolls vary daily according to a byzantine schedule but are usually 3 SP.

A toll collector might reduce or waive these tolls for travelers with paperwork from a Red Wizard or government official, or in exchange for a service or favor from adventurers. Conversely, corrupt tax collectors might impose "fees" on travelers to line their own pockets. Tolls are never higher than 1 GP.

#### Guild of Foreign Trade

Thay's Guild of Foreign Trade manages the nation's lucrative, high-volume exports of magic items. Based in the city of Bezantur, the Guild of Foreign Trade keeps enormous warehouses of primarily Common and Uncommon magic items to be sent to Thayan trade enclaves abroad. The guild is corrupt, and its business practices are immoral, but no group can rival the low prices or easy access to magic items Thay offers.

The guild's leader, a human Red Wizard named Gulga Vull (Medium Archmage), grew up an orphan on the streets of Bezantur, but her business acumen led to her meteoric rise. Vull focuses on maximizing profitability, hoping to expand Thayan trade dominance and therefore her own position as an adviser to the zulkirs.

## 3. Culture, Social Stratification, and Hidden Movements

In general, Thayans oppress all those beneath them on the social ladder. Working class Thayans suffer a life of hard labor. Most have adapted to such a life over generations. Still, some ponder a better way, and a few even dare to whisper of rebellion.

### 3.1 The Social Caste System

Thayan society is strictly stratified into three distinct socio-economic tiers. Upward mobility is virtually impossible outside of extraordinary arcane talent, military distinction, or organized crime:

1. **The Ruling Aristocracy (Red Wizards):** The absolute social elite composed of Red Wizards, their immediate families, scions, sages, and favored apprentices. They hold total legal impunity and control the nation's military and economic engine.
2. **The Middle Class:** Composed of bureaucrats, trade merchants, officers, artisans, and state-sanctioned priests. While living in relative comfort, their security depends on absolute deference to the ruling mages.

3. **The Working Class:** The massive foundation of Thay's population, consisting of laborers, farmers, miners, dockworkers, and indentured servants. Tied largely to their places of birth, they endure brutal physical labor and harsh taxation with zero legal protections.

### 3.2 Traditions of Survival: Closed Eyes vs. Open Eyes

To survive under the constant surveillance of Red Wizard informants and state scrying, ordinary Thayans adhere to two opposing cultural traditions:

### 3.3 Divine Worship in Thay

Religion in Thay is heavily shaped by political utility, personal ambition, and raw survival. While state authority discourages spiritual allegiance that competes with the Red Wizards, several pantheons flourish in the shadows:

- **Bane (God of Tyranny):** The most widespread and state-favored faith in Thay. His patronage over control, ambition, and authority appeals directly to Red Wizards, military commanders, and ambitious bureaucrats.
- **Cyric (God of Lies):** Immensely popular among urban commoners, covert operatives, and criminals who rely on deception, forgery, and false documentation to evade state enforcers.
- **Shar (God of Loss & Forgetfulness):** Revered by working-class Thayans seeking emotional numbness from personal grief, loss, and the bitter realities of life under magocratic rule.
- **Kossuth (Primordial of Fire):** Worshipped by destructive Red Wizards who embrace raw power and the absolute purification of rival factions through elemental flame.
- **Mask (God of Thieves):** The patron deity of highway smugglers, burglars, and the massive criminal underworld operating in commercial hubs like Bezantur.
- **Chauntea (Goddess of Agriculture):** Quietly revered by rural farming communities and agrarian laborers working the fertile volcanic soils of the lowlands.
- **Mystra (Goddess of Magic):** Generally avoided by commoners and disliked by Red Wizards (who prefer direct mastery over divine oversight). However, she is openly and legally worshipped in the autonomous free city of **Amruthar** under the leadership of Mayor Ulthara Toll.

These social dynamics and spiritual allegiances play out across a dramatically engineered physical landscape designed for maximum defense and municipal control.

## 4. Geography, Climate, and Key Locations

### 4.1 Tiered Geography & Unnatural Weather

Geographically, Thay resembles a massive, two-tiered wedding cake rising abruptly above the surrounding eastern realms. This massive natural elevation creates formidable defensive barriers against foreign invasion:

- **Lower Thay:** The lowest tier at sea level, curving along the western and southern coastlines. It features fertile agricultural fields, massive livestock herds, and crucial shipping channels. Because of its border access, Lower Thay serves as the strategic launching pad for foreign military campaigns. The land rises dramatically into the middle tier via the sheer cliffs of the **First Escarpment**.
- **The Thayan Plateau:** The vast, populous middle tier of the nation. It consists of rolling vistas, sprawling metropolitan centers, arid plains, and haunted Mulhorandi ruins. The plateau extends toward the center of the nation, where it terminates at the sheer precipices of the **Second Escarpment**.
- **Thaymount:** The highest, central volcanic plateau. Forbidden to all except high-ranking Red Wizards and their invited guests, Thaymount houses private magical research spires, undead-staffed fabrication plants, and Szass Tam’s massive subterranean stronghold, **The Citadel**.

#### Magically Induced Climate Anomalies

Centuries ago, the Red Wizards instituted permanent weather-controlling arcana across the arid landscape. While this ensures ample rainfall for crops across the volcanic soil, it results in frequent, unnatural atmospheric disruptions:

- Intense, sudden downpours occurring under bright, cloudless sunshine.
- Ominous, thick banks of crimson clouds lingering low over urban wards.
- Light, stinging drizzles that persist for hours despite bone-dry atmospheric air.
- Wispy clouds racing wildly across the sky during absolute, windless calms.

### 4.2 Eltabbar (The Capital)

Eltabbar is the sprawling capital city of Thay, serving as its primary political hub, administrative center, and primary seat of power for the Council of Zulkirs.

#### Demonic Legacy & Wild Magic Catacombs

Eltabbar’s distinct urban grid conceals a dark history. Centuries ago, Thayan mages allied with a demon lord named Eltab to overthrow Mulhorandi rule. Unable to banish the fiend afterward, Red Wizards engineered Eltabbar’s street grids, canals, and subterranean architecture into massive, precise arcane glyphs that bound Eltab in a subterranean prison.

Although Eltab has since escaped his bonds, the containment glyphs permanently altered the city's bedrock. The vast catacombs beneath Eltabbar roil with latent Abyssal energy, creating a volatile, city-wide **wild magic zone** infested with stray demons, aberrations, and rogue undead.

#### Eltabbar City Ward Breakdown

##### Primary Operational Wards

- **Zulkirs' Ward:** The breathtaking political apex of Thay. Features soaring spires topped with illusory waterfalls, private palatial estates for all eight Zulkirs, and suspended aerial chambers where state bureaucrats jockey for power.
- **Mages' Ward:** An elite district of gleaming mansions, scribes' workshops, and high-end arcana shops. While common street crime is nonexistent due to intense watch presence, sub-rosa assassinations between rival mages occur frequently.
- **Bazaar Ward:** The commercial heart of Eltabbar, built over central canals. Famous for "Market First Days" (the first day of each month), where high-fashion houses, exotic imports, and rare magic items are traded under tight security.
- **Apprentices' Ward:** An academic sector filled with grand libraries, reading rooms, and the prestigious **Tower of the Arcane**—Thay's premier state-sanctioned magic academy.
- **Warehouse Ward:** Situated along Lake Thaylambar, this bustling shipping district handles massive magic item exports operated by the Guild of Foreign Trade and major transport fronts.
- **Eltab's Ward:** A desolate, crime-ridden slum built directly over the subterranean demonic prison. Packed with cramped tenements and crooked taverns like the _Hidden Lord's Lair_, it serves as a breeding ground for underground rebel cells.

##### Secondary Operational Wards

- **Canal Ward:** A clean, residential ward housing bazaar workers, elite servants, and middle-class merchants.
- **Lower Ward:** A bustling middle-class district hosting long-term accommodations for foreign travelers, mercenaries, and visiting agents, including the famous **Crystal Ball Inn**.
- **Workshop Ward:** The heavy industrial district housing fabrication plants where magic items are mass-produced and spell scrolls are scribed by indentured workers.
- **Ward of Towers:** A dense concentration of high-level research spires, including _Mythrellaa's Tower of Illusions_, dedicated to developing grand rituals.

### 4.3 Notable Regional Tharchs

Outside the capital, Thay's remaining ten tharchs offer distinct regional flavors, political threats, and operational environments:

- **Priador:** The commercial heart of Thay. Features the massive port city of **Bezantur**, home to the Guild of Foreign Trade headquarters, expansive naval docks, and massive magic item fabrication plants.
- **Pyarados:** A rugged mining region along the Sunrise Mountains. Unscrupulous Red Wizards work living laborers to death in the mines and then raise them as undead to continue mining operations indefinitely.
- **Lapendrar:** A southwestern border province hosting the autonomous free city of **Amruthar**. Red Wizards hold no legal authority within Amruthar, making it a sanctuary for dissidents; however, city authorities require all entrants to survive a _Zone of Truth_ spell, and all deceased individuals are questioned via _Speak with Dead_ to prevent Thayan infiltration.
- **Surthay:** A cold, swampy northern backwater containing the fetid **Surmarsh**. Characterized by hostile lizardfolk clans under Sovereigns Vrill and Xornil Fangbane, and ongoing border skirmishes with Rashemen.
- **Gauros:** Rugged, monster-infested mining hills where Tharchion Ozgund Gar garrisons conscripts at **Fort Gar** against an active, gathering army of gnolls led by a Glabrezu named **Krazt**.

- **The Alaor:** An island tharch off the southern coast featuring deepwater harbors, massive shipyards, and the command center of the Thayan Navy.
- **Delhumide:** A rural province composed of cattle ranches and the ghost-haunted, cursed ruins of ancient Delhumide.
- **Thazalhar:** A heavily fortified river checkpoint (**Thay Crossing**) where state inspectors examine and heavily tax merchant caravans entering from Mulhorand.
- **Tyraturos:** The most populous mercantile tharch, crossed by the High Road and famous for the city of Tyraturos and its "hundred taverns."
- **Thaymount:** The forbidden volcanic central plateau housing Szass Tam's Citadel, the Dread Legion garrisons, and the exclusive Red Wizard resort complex of **Szul**.

Equipped with a comprehensive operational map of Thay's institutions, geography, and shadow conflicts, prospective agents can formulate their strategy for survival.

## 5. Player's Blueprint

### 5.1 Adventuring in Thay

Thay's reputation as a deadly land is well-earned. But heroes, especially those who wield magic, can achieve great heights here.

#### Traveling Adventurers

Adventurers from outside Thay might visit the nation for the following reasons:

- **Magic Item Retrieval.** Thay's tharchs are littered with ancient sites filled with lost relics and magic items, from the tombs of ancient Mulhorand god-kings in Pyarados to the ruins of the imperial city of Delhumide.
- **Magical Secrets.** Mages throughout Faerûn trace their ancestry to Thayans who fled the Red Wizards. Some might seek spellbooks, magic items, or other remnants of their heritage. A wealthy mage might hire the characters to retrieve an item from the ruins of their family's Thayan manor or from the Red Wizards who stole it.
- **Sabotage Mission.** Some powerful Red Wizards orchestrate nefarious international networks from Thay. A powerful patron might hire the characters to infiltrate Thay and unravel an evil Red Wizard's operation, preventing death and suffering across the world.

#### Thayan Adventurers

Within Thay, remarkable individuals become adventurers despite the Red Wizards' oppression, or adventurers might be Red Wizards themselves. Heroes from Thay might follow these paths:

- **Hero of the People.** The character knows that Thay's common folk suffer greatly and that those in need usually can't pay. The hero helps the downtrodden no matter the cost. They might rescue a prisoner unjustly sentenced to death or retrieve vital goods stolen from a village. Eventually, the Red Wizards confront the character, and it's up to the hero whether they want to fight back or find allies among the mages' ruthless ranks.
- **Open Eyes Operative.** The character might choose to operate as an Open Eyes rebel, sabotaging and undermining the Red Wizards. They might torch a Red Wizard's magic item shipment or steal from a mage's vault. If discovered, the character attracts the Red Wizards' ire; regardless, most locals refuse to associate with them for fear of reprisal.
- **Red Wizard.** The hero could be a Red Wizard seeking to overturn the status quo or fighting to rise in rank. A selfish and cruel Red Wizard isn't a good candidate to join an adventuring party, though perhaps an adventuring Red Wizard has left their unsavory past behind. For guidance on playing a Red Wizard character, see chapter 2.
- **Red Wizard's Agent.** Not all Red Wizards in Thay are evil, and no matter their predilections, many Red Wizards pay adventurers well to handle various tasks.

### 5.2 Spy Thriller Mechanics & Tactical Tradecraft

Executing covert missions inside Thay requires strict adherence to magical tradecraft. Careless mistakes compromise safehouses and draw swift execution by Szass Tam's loyalists. There are mechanical consequences at play if an operative or party violates operational secrecy.

- **Code Names & Cover Identities:** Operatives must use assigned group aliases (e.g., _Zero Team_, _Doppelgangers_) and individual callsigns (e.g., _The Crane_, _The Sparrow_, _Onyx_, _Ghost_) in public. Discussing real names or actual targets in public exposes the party to scrying and informants.
- **Magical Reconnaissance & Infiltration:** Knowledge and secrets may mean the difference between life and death for operatives, enemies, and allies. Skills, spells, and abilities that aid in subtle (or overt) communication are essential.
- **Identity Concealment:** Stealth and obfuscation will be key. Skills, spells, abilities, and items that conceal one's identity may mean the difference between success or catastrophe.

### 5.3 Death and Failure

In this campaign, "_Character Death_" may not be the result of a combat encounter. An operative(s) cover can be destroyed to the extent that it's impossible for them to continue. The same is true for "_Total Party Kill_".
